# Volume 2 / Act III Research — Bitcoin Scaling War & ICO Boom, 2017

## R2-A3-01 — SegWit proposal/deployment
- Segregated Witness is specified in BIP 141, assigned 2015-12-21 and deployed as a backward-compatible soft fork.
- Bitcoin Core 0.13.1 (2016-10-27) included mainnet activation parameters using BIP9 signaling.
- SegWit ultimately activated on Bitcoin mainnet at block 481,824 on 2017-08-24.
- Sources: bitcoin/bips BIP141; bitcoin.org Bitcoin Core 0.13.1 release; blockchain explorer for block 481,824.

## R2-A3-02 — New York Agreement / SegWit2x
- On 2017-05-23 Digital Currency Group published the `Bitcoin Scaling Agreement at Consensus 2017`.
- Signatories proposed two parallel steps: activate SegWit at an 80% signaling threshold and later conduct a 2 MB hard fork within six months.
- The agreement represented companies, miners and infrastructure operators, but did not constitute universal Bitcoin consensus.
- Source: Digital Currency Group 2017-05-23 agreement.

## R2-A3-03 — Bitcoin Cash split
- Bitcoin Cash activated a hard-fork rule set on 2017-08-01.
- The UAHF specification required the fork block to exceed 1 MB and configured an 8 MB block-size limit.
- The resulting chain became known as Bitcoin Cash; holders of BTC at the fork could have corresponding BCH on the new chain depending on custody/access arrangements.
- Sources: Bitcoin Cash Node documentation and UAHF technical specification; later SEC/Grayscale filings describing the 2017 BTC/BCH fork.

## R2-A3-04 — SegWit activation
- Bitcoin SegWit activated at block 481,824 on 2017-08-24.
- This did not merge Bitcoin Cash back into Bitcoin; the chains remained separate.
- Source: BIP141; Bitcoin block 481,824 explorer data.

## R2-A3-05 — SEC DAO Report and ICO regulation signal
- On 2017-07-25 the SEC released its DAO investigative report and stated that, under the facts and circumstances examined, DAO tokens were securities.
- The SEC emphasized that U.S. federal securities laws can apply regardless of whether an issuer is a traditional company or a decentralized organization, and regardless of whether tokens are purchased with fiat or virtual currency.
- The same day Investor.gov published an ICO bulletin warning that ICOs could present legitimate opportunities but also fraud and high-risk offerings.
- Sources: SEC Press Release 2017-131; SEC DAO Report; Investor.gov `Initial Coin Offerings` bulletin.

## R2-A3-06 — 2017 speculative peak and reversal
- Reuters reported Bitcoin rising from below $1,000 at the start of 2017 to as high as $19,666 on Bitstamp on 2017-12-17, with some exchanges above $20,000.
- On 2017-12-22 Bitcoin fell as low as roughly $11,159 before partially recovering, producing its worst week since 2013 at the time.
- Sources: Reuters reports republished by multiple outlets, Dec. 22–26 2017.

## Act III 창작 경계
- `Bitcoin Cash`, `Big Blocks`, `Small Blocks`, `Miners`, `Nodes`, `Businesses`, `ICO` may be personified as composite actors. Their dialogue is C2.
- The scaling conflict had many factions; do not reduce it to developers vs miners or companies vs users alone.
- The New York Agreement was influential but not a binding constitution for the network.
- Bitcoin Cash's creation and SegWit's activation are separate events.
- Do not state that every ICO was fraudulent or every token was a security. Regulatory status depends on facts and circumstances.
- 2017 prices are exchange-specific; use approximate values and identify Bitstamp/other exchange differences when needed.
